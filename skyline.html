Skyline Robotics

A B2B fleet monitoring platform for robotic window cleaning

Skyline Robotics builds autonomous robots that clean the windows of skyscrapers. The robots collected a lot of operational data, but the company had no product that let its clients use it.

As the only designer, I designed that product from the ground up: a B2B SaaS platform and its design system, built for three users with different needs. Cleaning company owners track what the fleet saves them, area managers follow every building in real time, and building managers get performance reports.

The challenge

Problem

Lacking an interface, stakeholders were “blind” to operations, hindering oversight and granular control.

While robots cleaned buildings across the city, stakeholders remained operationally blind in the office. Without a high-level view of the fleet, they couldn’t detect performance deviations or provide the proof of work needed to justify robotics over manual labor.

Pain points

  • No big-picture view of fleet activity, leading to total reliance on verbal reports.
  • Inability to detect recurring issues or deviations from the cleaning schedule.
  • No data-driven evidence to prove the robots’ efficiency compared to manual labor.
  • Critical business data was trapped in engineering logs, inaccessible to decision-makers.

The solution

Solution

A hierarchical platform transforming real-time data into strategic oversight and direct field control.

A unified platform that gives stakeholders a single source of truth. It answers the three most critical questions: Is the fleet working? Are there recurring issues? And is the investment paying off? It also gives managers the real-time control to keep daily operations on schedule.

Key features

  • Real-time fleet status: instant visibility across all sites.
  • Recurring trend analysis: identifying systematic issues before they escalate.
  • Automated value metrics: live tracking of savings and operational efficiency.
  • Deep-dive hierarchy: a seamless move from the city view to a single robot.

Success criteria

  • Self-serve insights
  • Reduced support overhead
  • Lower operational risk

Information architecture

Defining system flows: bridging user needs through information architecture

To serve both the company owner’s big-picture needs and the area manager’s operational detail, I designed a four-level drill-down: from a Global Fleet Overview (level 1) to a single building’s window-level status (level 3) and its analytics (level 4). Data is never overwhelming, and fragmented engineering logs became one actionable tool.

Key learnings

  • Visibility firstPrioritizing “what is happening now” over historical analysis was the key to adoption.
  • Dual persona designServing both operational detail (area managers) and an executive overview (company owners) needed a flexible drill-down architecture.
  • Operational efficiencyPutting weather and wind data on the map let managers decide on weather stops without switching tools.
  • System standardizationA modular design system let the platform grow its data architecture even while the product was in rapid MVP stages.
